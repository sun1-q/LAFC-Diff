# LAFC-Diff
Lesion-Aware and Frequency-Constrained
Diffusion for Cross-Modal Retinal Image Translation
ABSTRACT: Fundus Fluorescein Angiography (FA) is a vital tool for diagnosing fundus vascular diseases like Diabetic Retinopathy (DR). However, this invasive examination carries potential risks. Recently, deep learning-based cross-modal image generation provides a new approach to synthesize FA images from safe Fundus Structure (FS) images. Yet, existing generative models struggle with weakly registered fundus images and often fail to preserve the continuity of capillary terminals. Furthermore, large normal background areas completely dominate these images. As a result, traditional global optimization mechanisms easily overlook minute lesions like microaneurysms and dot hemorrhages, causing severe missed diagnoses or rendering errors. To address this issue, a lesion-aware and frequency-constrained adversarial diffusion network is proposed to improve the generation of tiny lesions, such as microaneurysms. Building upon the adversarial diffusion model, a high-frequency residual constraint is first introduced. This explicitly aligns high-frequency features in the frequency domain to enhance the continuity and edge sharpness of the capillary network. Second, a brightness-adaptive weighting mechanism is incorporated. This dynamically assigns larger optimization weights to high-brightness pixels, improving the generation of lesion regions. Additionally, a local lesion-aware mechanism is introduced to guide the network in accurately attending to lesion regions. This effectively elevates the generation quality of minute lesions. An ablation study and comparison experiments against various algorithms are conducted on the public Isfahan MISP dataset. The proposed model achieves superior results across the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM), Peak Signal-to-Noise Ratio (PSNR), and Learned Perceptual Image Patch Similarity (LPIPS) metrics. Specifically, it attains an average SSIM of 0.693, a PSNR of 18.84, and an LPIPS of 0.294, outperforming state-of-the-art (SOTA) algorithms. This demonstrates that the proposed model enables cross-modal translation of non-invasive fundus images and effectively addresses the issue of losing small lesions against complex backgrounds.
